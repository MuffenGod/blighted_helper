## 1. Design decisions (before any building)

1. **Keep the dragon.** The jester is a dragon girl in costume, so horns, tail and wings stay. This also keeps all animations valid, because bone names stay the same.
2. **Costume language:**
   - A **2-point or 3-point jester hat** with bells, worn between or around the horns.
   - A **harlequin diamond** pattern (two-colour checks) on the bodice and the skirt.
   - A **ruffled neck ruff** (collar).
   - **Asymmetric legs and sleeves**: left half one colour, right half the other (motley).
   - **Pointed curled shoes**, with bells.
   - **Clown face paint**: diamond over one eye, red nose or cheek dots, painted smile.
3. **Palette** (keeps her lock's black and gold as anchors, so she still reads as Emily):

| Role | Colour |
|---|---|
| Primary A | deep crimson `(176,32,56)` |
| Primary B | black `(30,24,34)`, from her lock |
| Accent | gold `(232,192,82)`, bells and trim |
| Pop | emerald `(58,196,112)`, kept for the gem and eyes only |
| Ruff and face | cream `(252,236,220)` |
| Face paint | white `(250,250,250)` and red `(210,30,50)` |

4. **Silhouette first.** From 8 blocks away, you should see: hat points, ruff, checker dress, big wings. Anything that does not help that read gets cut.

**Budget:** current model has 136 bones. I add about 40 to 50 bones (hat, pigtails, ruff, toes). That is fine, since the study shows 195 bones and 490 cubes run normally.

**Rules while building:**
- Keep each new bone's **pivot at its joint** (hat tips at the band, toes at the ankle), or animation will swing from the wrong point.
- Use **inflate and rotation**, not axis-aligned boxes, as the study recommends.
- Keep cube UV faces unique so the checker can differ left to right.
- No new geometry in the **hitbox area**: Emily's collision and `visible_bounds` (8 x 5) stay. The hat points may go above the head, so I will raise `visible_bounds_height` if the hat is clipped.

### Step 4. Animations
1. Start with `jester.animation.json`, a copy of `emily.animation.json` (the bone names match, so everything still works on day one).
2. Add keyframes for the new bones to existing animations:
   - `idle` and `idle_hair`: hat points and pigtails sway with a phase offset. Bells swing about 10 degrees.
   - `walk` and `sway_walk`: shoes' toe chains bounce, the ruff bobs slightly.
   - `attack`: hat tips whip back.
3. New short animation `animation.jester.giggle` (head bob, shoulders shake) for use on the existing `expr_grin` trigger.
4. Procedural Molang is skipped (GeckoLib handles hand-keyed animation fine, and this keeps it simple).

## 3. Step-by-step: texture

The texture is 512x1024 with a matching glowmask, so everything is painted on a fresh copy.

### Step 5. Make the UV templates
1. Export a **UV map overlay** from `build_jester.js` (labelled islands per bone). If the generator has no such mode, I add a small function that draws each face's rectangle with its bone name.
2. Copy `emily.png` to `jester.png`, `emily_glowmask.png` to `jester_glowmask.png`.

### Step 6. Painting order (back to front)
1. **Base fills by island** with palette colours: body skin untouched, dress crimson, one sleeve black, opposite sleeve crimson, legs split.
2. **Checker pattern.** A script (in PowerShell or in the Node generator, since there is no Python on this machine) fills each dress/wing/tail island with diamonds. Diamond size is 4 to 6 pixels, so it stays readable at 512 px. The pattern is aligned per island so seams meet.
3. **Shading.** Five-step tonal ramp per hue, as the study recommends: shadow under the ruff, under bust, inside skirt folds, wing membrane near the bones.
4. **Outline.** Dark 1 px line on the darkest ramp step around every island so it stays crisp.
5. **Gold trim and bells.** Gold (232,192,82) with a highlight pixel, and a dark gold shadow.
6. **Face.**
   - Keep the existing fair skin and green slit-pupil eyes (the lock rule).
   - White face paint over the lower face.
   - Red diamond over the left eye, small black diamond under the right.
   - Red nose dot and small cheek circles.
   - Wide painted red smile that follows the fang smile.
   - **Warning:** the lock mentions skin going grey, so I keep a colour check on the face island after every pass.
7. **Ruff:** cream with grey-lavender folds.
8. **Hat:** split-colour (crimson and black) tips, gold band with diamond studs.

### Step 7. Glow mask
Paint only: eyes, the green gem, the bells (a soft gold glow), and optionally the face-paint diamonds. Black elsewhere. ## 4. Step-by-step: wiring it into the mod

1. **`HelperVariant`:** add `JESTER(4, "Harlequin", "Chaos Trickster", titleColor 0xE03050, glowColor 0xFFD54F, texture/glow paths, description, magic specialty, footnote, name pool: Harley, Pepper, Jinx, Giggles, Pierrette, Joker, Belle, Motley)`. Check `byId` and anywhere that switches on the variant so the enum change causes no gaps (for example the one in `HelperGaiaModel` lines 287+, the starter screen, and the `/helper` commands in `HelperCommands`).
2. **`HelperGeoModel`:** `getModelResource`, `getTextureResource` and `getAnimationResource` switch on `animatable.getVariant()`: jester gets `jester.*`, everyone else keeps the current resources. (Today it returns constants, so this is the key code change.)
3. **`HelperGeoRenderer`:** the glow layer picks `jester_glowmask.png` for the jester.
4. **Gameplay:** decide the jester's role. Suggestion: a **debuff and crowd-control** specialty (blind, confuse, bell stun) using the existing spell structure in `EmilySpells` and `HelperCasts`, so she has a reason to exist beyond looks. Optional for the first pass.
5. **Lang, knowledge and standee:** add strings in `lang/en_us.json`. Skip `standee/layers.json` unless you want a jester standee.
6. **Save compatibility:** the variant is stored as an id, so adding id 4 is safe. Old saves are unaffected.

## 5. Testing and iteration loop

1. `build_jester.js` gives the new geo and texture. Gradle build copies the resources. A hot reload via F3+T checks the texture quickly without restarting.
2. Check in-game: front, side, back, standing, walking, attacking, kneeling, and with each gear item (curios and armor layers in `CuriosLayerAdder`, which may clip with the hat).
3. Check glow at night.
4. Fix: clipping (hat vs horns and helmets), seams in the checker pattern, bells' pivots, the skirt in `walk`.
5. Save screenshots to `design/helper_shots` as before and compare with the concept sheet.
6. Rebuild the jar and install it the same way you did for the 1.5.0 backup flow.
